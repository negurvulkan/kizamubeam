# Disc sphere

The **Disc sphere** (**Toolbox → 3D slot-together models**) builds a sphere or lamp shade from round discs. The discs hold each other through angled slots, without glue.

The discs sit on the face directions of a polyhedron, chosen under **Layout**. More discs make a rounder sphere with more slots; the icosahedron is the default.

| Polyhedron | Discs |
|---|---|
| Cube | 6 |
| Octahedron | 8 |
| Dodecahedron | 12 |
| Cuboctahedron | 14 |
| Icosahedron | 20 |
| Rhombicuboctahedron | 26 |
| Football | 32 |

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Diameter** | Outer diameter of the finished sphere. |
| **Overlap (%)** | 0 % = the discs just interlock, 100 % = as far as possible without three discs meeting in one point. More overlap gives larger discs, a firmer hold and more pattern. |
| **Material thickness** | Thickness of the material. |
| **Top opening** | Hole in the top disc, for example for a lamp socket (E14 about 30 mm, E27 about 40 mm); 0 = none. |
| **Bottom opening** | Hole in the bottom disc, for example to change the bulb (Advanced). |
| **Fit clearance** | Extra slot width; negative = press fit. |
| **Kerf** | Width of the laser cut. |

Each pair of crossing discs shares a chord that is split in half: one disc is slotted from one end, the other from the other end. Slots for discs that cross at an angle are made wider accordingly, and sharp corners at the slot entrances get a small chamfer.

## Material and assembly

Thin, slightly flexible material works best: paper, card, polypropylene or veneer. The discs have to bend a little when the last ones are slotted in. Start with the discs around one point and work your way around the sphere.

For a lamp, use LED bulbs only: they stay cool. Keep paper and wood well away from the bulb.

The 3D view shows the assembled sphere and checks that the discs do not collide.
